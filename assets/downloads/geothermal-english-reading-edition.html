Geothermal Cascade Utilisation

Recover usable heat in successive stages rather than discarding warm geothermal water after one application.

Source page 22

Cascade Utilisation Flow Diagram

Production well → high-temperature uses, 80-60°C: thermal driving of liquid-desiccant dehumidification, bathing-pool heating and domestic hot-water heating → intermediate uses, ≤60°C: play-pool make-up water and temperature maintenance, low-temperature make-up water heating and floor/air-conditioning heating → low-temperature uses, 40-20°C: water-source heat-pump recovery and heating → reinjection, ≤20°C. Temperature bands are illustrative source values, not fixed design setpoints.

Source page 23

Water-Source Heat-Pump Operation

Electrical input enables a heat pump to transfer low-grade source heat to a higher useful temperature. Cooling: compressor → condenser, rejecting heat to groundwater/cooling water → expansion valve → evaporator, absorbing heat from building chilled water → compressor. Heating: the condenser heats building water; the expansion valve reduces refrigerant pressure; the evaporator extracts source-water heat; vapour returns to the compressor.

Closed and Open Single-Well Systems

Closed-loop example: double-U HDPE pipes, water or antifreeze, and circulation pump. Source shallow examples: 80-150 m depth and 10-25°C ground temperature. Winter: collect ground heat and upgrade with a heat pump; summer: reject building heat to the ground. Open-loop example: pump groundwater through a plate exchanger and reinject through the same or a nearby well. Source calls for full same-aquifer reinjection. Shallow U-tube and deep fracture-enhanced systems are different technologies.

Geothermal Scale Control

Source page 46

Why Geothermal Systems Scale

Calcium, silica, magnesium and iron, plus dissolved CO₂, oxygen and H₂S, may be present in geothermal fluids. Pressure, temperature and gas changes can cause precipitation. Source lists calcium carbonate, calcium silicate, silica, calcite and barite deposits. Hardened deposits restrict flow and production. Deposit and water analysis inform treatment.

Source page 47

Scale Formation - Flow Diagram

Geothermal fluid rises → pressure/temperature change. Pressure falls → CO₂ release → pH rises → carbonate concentration rises → supersaturated calcium carbonate precipitates → pipe-wall scale. Temperature falls → silica solubility falls → silicic-acid polymerisation → colloidal silica deposition → scale. Corrosion products/microorganisms provide nucleation sites. Key: CO₂逸出 = CO₂ release; 硅酸聚合 = silicic-acid polymerisation; 附着管壁成垢 = pipe-wall deposition; 提供成核位点 = provide nucleation sites.

Source page 48

Treatment Decision Flowchart - Source Mismatch

Source title says scale removal, but the internal diagram concerns corrosion. Full flow: water analysis → dominant corrosion cause. Dissolved oxygen → oxygen scavenger + passivator. Chloride/H₂S → adsorbing imidazoline + molybdate. Low-temperature acidic water → neutraliser + organic amine. High-temperature alkaline water → dispersing scale inhibitor + zinc salt. Dynamic dosing → corrosion coupons + electrochemical monitoring → corrosion rate ≤0.1 mm/year? Yes: effective; no: adjust blend/dose. Source options are not validated chemical prescriptions.

Source page 51

Corrosion Mechanisms

Four interacting contributors: electrochemical corrosion in conductive water, chloride/sulphide attack on passive films; acidic species associated with CO₂/H₂S; oxygen ingress and differential-aeration/crevice attack; and microbiologically influenced corrosion. Temperature, salinity, gases, oxygen and microbiology must be assessed together. Numerical temperature/rate generalisations in the source are not universal design rules.
