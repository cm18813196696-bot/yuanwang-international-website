When the warranty starts

Normally the warranty begins on signed commissioning and acceptance at the destination site, after the fifth commissioning checkpoint. If acceptance is delayed by the customer for more than 30 days, the warranty start date moves to the date the equipment arrived on site. This arrangement must be reflected in the project contract.

Exclusions and maintenance requirements
  • Installation, operation or maintenance contrary to the manuals, including omitted flushing, missed backwashing and incorrect electrical connections.
  • Water outside the design water-quality schedule attached to the contract, including damaging scaling, corrosion, iron, manganese, sulphur or chloride conditions.
  • Overdue maintenance. Where the agreed warranty incorporates this condition, failure to complete required annual maintenance reduces the original warranty period by half.
  • Human damage, misuse, unapproved modifications and operation outside the approved conditions.
  • Force-majeure events such as earthquake, flood, extreme freezing, lightning, war or epidemic restrictions.
  • Normal wear and natural changes in appearance.

Keep dated maintenance and water-quality records so that the cause and applicable cover can be established.

Annual system health report

Yuanwang or an authorised service provider prepares a report covering annual operating trends, efficiency change against the first-year baseline, component-life estimates, replacement recommendations and next-year maintenance or upgrade priorities.

The report gives owners a documented condition review that may support operational, insurance or financing discussions. The scope and any third-party acceptance requirements should be agreed separately.

Geothermal wells: additional records and early warnings
  • Create a well record at handover: depth, diameter, aquifer, initial water level, temperature, yield, sand content and mineralisation.
  • Define acceptance at an agreed operating point, including yield, temperature and drawdown. Record the actual values rather than leaving these conditions unspecified.
  • Compare annual results with the initial baseline. A 15% decline in yield triggers a diagnostic review of screen blockage, scaling, pump wear and regional groundwater changes.
  • Assess well-pump replacement after approximately 5–8 years according to condition and duty. Qualified personnel must carry out the work; Yuanwang can assist with selection and supervision.
  • Consider online level, temperature and flow monitoring, with an agreed route for returning the data for early assessment.

08 / CLAIMS & COMMERCIAL TERMS

Evidence first. A documented decision.

A warranty claim should include equipment serial number and proof of purchase; a description and timing of the fault; photographs or video; operating and water-quality records; and evidence of required maintenance. Incomplete claims may be held pending the missing information.

Assessment order, notification and timing

Responsibility is assessed in the following order:

  1. Contract and attachments, including design duty, water-quality conditions and acceptance criteria.
  2. The five installation and commissioning checkpoint records.
  3. Operating and maintenance logs.
  4. Examination of the failed part and any independent test report.
  5. Relevant industry practice and original manufacturer standards.

This policy proposes notice within 30 days after discovering, or being expected to discover, a defect. This is a proposed contractual notification period, not a fixed deadline imposed by CISG Article 39. Where the CISG applies, Article 39 uses a reasonable-time requirement and generally a two-year outer limit from actual handover, subject to the contractual guarantee period and applicable exceptions.

Yuanwang’s response follows the priority table above. The target for a written responsibility assessment is 30 business days after complete claim materials are received.

Liability and dispute arrangements

The recommended contract arrangement is a project aggregate liability cap equal to the contract value and exclusion of indirect loss, lost profit, business interruption and third-party claims, except for deliberate misconduct, gross negligence and personal injury. These are proposed B2B contract terms; they apply only as agreed and to the extent legally enforceable.

The proposed governing law is the law of the People’s Republic of China, excluding Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan for this choice of law. The proposed forum is CIETAC arbitration in Beijing, with Chinese and/or English as agreed. Confirm governing law, seat, institution and language when signing; undisputed obligations continue during a dispute.

Payment, currency, tax and travel deposits
  • Services and spare parts normally use agreed prepayment. Allow for cross-border payment processing and intermediary charges; allow approximately 3–7 business days, with actual timing dependent on banks.
  • USD or EUR pricing is suggested. Specify who bears exchange-rate movements and which rate is used.
  • Destination duties and VAT normally fall to the buyer / importer, except under an agreed DDP allocation. Quotations should state whether destination taxes are excluded.
  • For an on-site visit, the suggested arrangement is a 50% advance against estimated travel expenses, followed by settlement against actual agreed costs.
Force majeure and changed circumstances

Relevant events can include war, civil disorder, terrorism, government restrictions, natural disasters, epidemic or quarantine restrictions, visa refusals or entry restrictions, shipping disruption, port strikes, export controls, sanctions, embargoes and destination-law changes preventing lawful supply.

The suggested contract arrangement provides for written notice and supporting evidence within 15 days; the parties then agree a revised plan. If the event continues for more than 6 months, either party may terminate the unperformed portion, with completed work settled on an actual basis. Any relief depends on the signed terms and applicable law.
